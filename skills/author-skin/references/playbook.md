# Authoring playbook

## Read the prototype before choosing a medium

List three to six fidelity features, ordered by what makes this skin
recognizable at a 5–15 px game cell: silhouette/head cue, dominant rhythm,
palette behavior, material, signature mark, and motion. Do not chase incidental
lighting or background artifacts.

Choose the cheapest faithful route, not the prototype's suggested route:

- `layers`: checkerboards, stripes, bands, geometric motifs, clean gradients,
  palette-aware patterns, shine, pulses, and formula-driven effects;
- `texture`: organic, painterly, illustrative, irregular, or materially rich
  static art that shapes would visibly flatten;
- `sprite_sheet`: motion whose distinct drawn frames matter—morphs, flowing
  illustrative material, frame-specific glints—not motion a time expression
  already expresses cleanly;
- `hybrid`: an image carries texture or drawn motion while v2 layers carry
  role cues, contour, fallback, head/tail treatment, highlights, or effects.

Probe the difficult feature first. If it disproves the prototype hint, change
the plan before paid asset work and state the evidence in `rationale` and
`risks`. Never escalate to pixels merely because they are easy to request.

## Turn the route into a plan

Write the plan before assets. Give every fidelity feature a layer or asset
owner. For each asset, choose natural X length from the mark scale and the
short/median/long body fixtures. If it repeats, prefer a repeat long enough that
distinctive mark clusters do not appear several times on a median snake.

For animation, use the document clock once. Prefer several simple effect layers
over one opaque raster animation when the motions need independent tuning. Use
a sheet when frame identity matters. Choose Y for temporal smoothness including
slow motion; choose X independently. Put a calm, complete version at row zero.

Plan a fallback directly below every image layer. It should retain team reading,
head reading, and enough of the design to look intentional while art loads.

## Iterate against evidence

After deterministic checks, compare real-render captures to the prototype in
fidelity-feature order. Repair the smallest owner of the largest miss. Keep
layer topology fixed, keep each asset attempt, and stop at the configured pass
budget. A miss caused by absent vocabulary is a platform gap, not a reason to
hide complexity in Rust or an oversized image.

Use feedback literally on the linked retry. Do not convert one rejection into
a global rule; technique promotion is the mechanism for knowledge that proves
itself across structurally different fixtures.
